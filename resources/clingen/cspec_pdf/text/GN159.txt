Criteria & Strength Specifications

PVS1
Original ACMG
Summary
Null variant (nonsense, frameshift, canonical +/−1 or 2 splice sites, initiation codon,
single or multi-exon deletion) in a gene where loss of function (LOF) is a known
mechanism of disease.
Caveats:
• Beware of genes where LOF is not a known disease mechanism (e.g. GFAP, MYH7).
• Use caution interpreting LOF variants at the extreme 3’ end of a gene.
• Use caution with splice variants that are predicted to lead to exon skipping but leave

the remainder of the protein intact.
• Use caution in the presence of multiple transcripts.
VCEP
ClinGen Myeloid Malignancy Variant Curation Expert Panel (MMSpecifications:VCEP) Notes:
1. The MM-VCEP recommends using NM_016222.4 as the default
transcript, which is the MANE Select transcript. This transcript
encodes a 622-amino acid protein. We note that there are other
transcripts described in the NCBI/RefSeq and Ensembl databases,
most of which are expected to lead to nonsense-mediated decay,
often due to retained introns. From the NCBI, NM_001321732.2 and
NM_001321830.2 encode 496-amino acid isoforms. Based on
expressed sequence tags, NM_016222.4 is very likely to be the
predominant transcript – with NM_001321732.2 likely representing a
transcript with intron 1 retention – and the other two have little
supportive evidence of significant expression (UCSC Genome
Browser). From Ensembl, ENST00000629036.2 encodes a 201-amino
acid isoform and ENST00000625286.1 encodes a 49-amino acid
isoform, but these are disregarded due to the significant lack of the
functional domains compared to the MANE Select isoform.
2. Alternate start sites in the NM_016222.4 transcript may occur at
Met127, Met132, or Met155. Protein expression of DDX41 in HEK cells
revealed the presence of a full-length protein (70 kDa) that was
primarily found in the nucleus and a smaller protein (~55 kDa) that
was primarily found in the cytoplasm and would align with a start site
at either Met127 or Met132. An expression level of 90% and 10% for
the p70 and p55 isoforms, respectively, was approximated. However,
this assessment was done in the context of overexpression, and it is
of note that the Kozak sequence around the alternate start sites
might be considered to be weaker than the Kozak sequence around
Met1, so the expression ratios may vary endogenously. Furthermore,
there is sufficient evidence of pathogenicity of variants upstream of
Met127 suggesting that a transcript with an alternate start site is
unlikely to be sufficient to compensate (possibly due to disrupted
nuclear localization) (PMID: 26712909).
3. Frameshift variation causing loss of the canonical stop, resulting in an
elongated protein, is more likely to be pathogenic due to induction of
non-stop decay and its disruptive downstream effects.
All protein extension variants due to frameshift alterations were
determined to result in an alternative stop codon beyond the
poly-A insertion site; thus, they are expected to be subject to
non-stop decay (see DDX41 PVS1 non-stop decay Figure).
Non-stop decay is another mRNA surveillance mechanism that
triggers degradation of an mRNA if protein translation is not
progressing at a normal rate, which is likely a result of

translation of the poly-A tail (series of lysine residues) that leads
to ribosomal stalling and collisions. There may be additional
causes of non-stop decay that are not well-described at this
time. (PMID: 12210514, 32688260, 36825529) Non-stop decay
has been described as a mechanism to consider in the curation
of potential null variants by the Association for Clinical Genomic
Science (ACGS), UK (https://www.acgs.uk.com/media/12533/ukpractice-guidelines-for-variant-classification-v12-2024.pdf) –
notably at a very strong strength level. Though non-stop decay
has not been included in the ClinGen SVI guidance for loss-offunction alterations (PMID: 30192042), a strength level of PVS1
was assigned as proposed in the recommendation by the ACGS
and as supported by internal analysis of genes that may
undergo NSD.
There is only one possible stop codon in the last exon of DDX41,
located at c.1867. Therefore, the MM-VCEP predicts that all -1
frameshift variants that escape NMD would undergo NSD. All the
+1 frameshift variants after c.1897 would also undergo NSD.
However, the resulting protein extension will depend on the
sequence of any random insertion. To determine whether a
variant results in a protein extension, the MM-VCEP advises that
curators check the cDNA using Mutalyzer (https://mutalyzer.nl/).
Mutalyzer displays the protein prediction with a “?” at the end to
indicate that the canonical stop codon is not in the coding
sequence or the 3’UTR (p.XXX###XXXfs*?), meaning the
alternative stop codon will be beyond the poly-A site
of DDX41. Mutalyzer also displays the affected protein predicted
sequence if the alternative stop codon is past the poly-A site,
allowing curators to compare the natural sequence including the
canonical stop codon, and the extended protein sequence, to
determine if a protein extension occurs. We have included
several example variants that result in NSD. Example frameshift
variants include:
Single nucleotide deletion: c.1658del
Double nucleotide deletion: c.1778_1779del
Four nucleotide deletion: c.1657_1660del
Single nucleotide duplication: c.1780dup
Double nucleotide duplication: c.1659_1660dup
4. Splicing effects are currently based solely on predictions. The rules
can be modified in the future if RNA evidence becomes available per
PMID: 37352859 as a guide. Strength modifications should be based
on the quality of the RNA study where:
Comparison to a control is necessary;
Patient RNA data are better than minigene assays;
Primers are designed to capture the possibility of multiexonic/multi-cassette events;

NMD inhibitors (e.g., puromycin, proprietary molecule found in
PAXgene tubes), particularly when the predicted effect is
nonsense-mediated decay, are used;
Quantification of the effect by SNP analysis is better than
percent splicing index (PSI) analysis is better than capillary
electrophoresis, which in turn, is better than estimation by gel
band density;
Multiple studies are better than a single study;
Caution should be used in modifying strength when the effect of
the splicing impact is incomplete (i.e.,"leaky" splice site) or
unclear.
5. The deleterious effects of copy number variants (CNVs)
in DDX41 (see DDX41 PVS1 decision tree for CNVs), most
frequently observed as large multi-exon deletions, are primarily
driven by disruption of exons encoding constrained residues, those
encoding P/LP missense variants. The presence of these P/LP
missense variants throughout the entire gene body indicate that all
protein regions are important for its function, and therefore, any outof-frame exon deletions are inherently deleterious due to the
resulting frameshift variant. Because there are no P/LP missense
variants encoded by exons 2, 4, or 15, we cannot assess the impact
of single exon deletions of those in-frame exons, and therefore, we
cannot apply PVS1. Any other in-frame deletion would involve an
exon encoding a P/LP missense variant, and therefore, we will apply
PVS1.

Original ACMG
Summary
De novo (both maternity and paternity confirmed) in a patient with the disease and no

Note: Confirmation of paternity only is insufficient. Egg donation, surrogate motherhood,
errors in embryo transfer, etc. can contribute to non-maternity.

Not Applicable
Comments:
DDX41 Specification: Do not use: The use of de novo cases is not
informative for DDX41, because no such cases have been identified.
Moreover, P/LP germline DDX41 variants cause late-onset cancers, after

reproductive years. Therefore, the MM-VCEP recommends that this rule
should not be applied for DDX41.

PS3
Original ACMG
Summary
Well-established in vitro or in vivo functional studies supportive of a damaging effect on
the gene or gene product.
Note: Functional studies that have been validated and shown to be reproducible and
robust in a clinical diagnostic laboratory setting are considered the most well-established.

Not Applicable
Comments:
DDX41 Specification: Do not use: The mechanism of tumorigenesis is
established as loss-of-function based on studies in cell lines or animal
models (PMID: 25920683, 35303436, 33763998). While recent studies
suggest that the mechanism may be related to the ability of R-loops – 3stranded RNA-DNA hybrids that play a role in transcription, DNA repair,
telomere maintenance, and chromosomal segregation – to drive genomic
instability (PMID: 34916496), the actual mechanism is still unknown. *See

PS4
Original ACMG
Summary
The prevalence of the variant in affected individuals is significantly increased compared to
the prevalence in controls.
Note 1: Relative risk (RR) or odds ratio (OR), as obtained from case-control studies, is
>5.0 and the confidence interval around the estimate of RR or OR does not include 1.0.
See manuscript for detailed guidance.
Note 2: In instances of very rare variants where case-control studies may not reach
statistical significance, the prior observation of the variant in multiple unrelated patients
with the same phenotype, and its absence in controls, may be used as moderate level of
evidence.
VCEP
MM-VCEP Notes:
Specifications:
1. The MM-VCEP has assessed the hematopoietic malignancies (HMs)
that have been published in those with pathogenic/likely pathogenic
DDX41 variants. As is widely accepted, these alleles are more
commonly present in myeloid malignancies, such as MDS and AML,
and much less frequently in lymphoid malignancies. For this reason,
our phenotypic definition of DDX41-related malignancies is based on
the occurrence of MDS/AML.

2. An aggregated synthetic cohort of DDX41 (DDX41-ASC) germline and
somatic variants was developed to inform the development of
DDX41-specific curation guidelines, specifically to refine case
enrichment for phenotypic ACMG/AMP criteria (PMID: 41703027). The
cohort was derived from 1796 people with HMs and cytopenias
compared to controls collected from three population databases:
gnomAD v4.1.0, ToMMo 54KJPN v20230626, and Korean Variant
Archive v2. From the DDX41-ASC, the highest portion of individuals
with germline P/LP variants were among those people diagnosed with
MDS/AML (79%), followed by those with unexplained cytopenias
(45%), lymphoid neoplasms (29%), and other myeloid neoplasms
(26%). A quasi-case-control study of specific variants in MDS/AMLenriched cases was performed due to the strong association of
germline DDX41 variants and MDS/AML diagnosis, and odds ratios
were calculated, with adjustments made for ancestry, to determine
PS4 criterion strengths.
3. Patients with DDX41 related hematopoietic malignancy predisposition
syndrome display incomplete penetrance and the median age of
onset of DDX41 related HMs is about 66 years (PMID: 34349893).
4. To further assess the age of onset in DDX41 related malignancies, the
MM-VCEP amassed a patient cohort of over 900 patients from
various DDX41 related publications and clinical data from
participating labs (PMID: 34349893, 35671390, 36322930). The MMVCEP analyzed age of onset and gender, finding that there was no
significant difference in age of onset between men and women, with
~97% of patients acquiring a DDX41-related malignancy at or over
40 years of age. Thus, full penetrance is not expected at an early
age. This was recapitulated using the DDX41-ASC (PMID: 41703027).

VCEP
MM-VCEP Notes:
Specifications:
1. The DDX41 germline predisposition syndrome does not impact
reproductive rates and has late disease penetrance, resulting in a
high allele frequency in the general population, including for the
most common deleterious variants (0.0174% for c.3G>A and
0.0201% for p.Asp140Glyfs*2 in gnomAD v4.1).
2. While setting an arbitrary threshold of 0.02% could allow for applying
the PM2_Supporting criterion to less common deleterious germline
DDX41 variants, the majority (98%) of DDX41 variants in the
gnomAD 4.1 database would also qualify.
3. Therefore, we only apply this criterion when the variant is absent
from the general population, recognizing that some variants may
become impacted as population databases grow.

2. Protein extension variants (see DDX41 PM4 decision tree Figure):
Stop-loss variants (DDX41 PM4 decision tree Figure,
left):
For substitutions: TAILVAR, an in-silico tool, evaluates
hydrophobicity, protein stability, and evolutionary
constraint of the extended C-terminus, with scores ≥0.70
predicting pathogenic outcomes (PMID:41556340). The
MM-VCEP therefore recommends the use of TAILVAR, with
scores ≥0.70 meeting PM4 criteria, for stop-loss
substitution variants in DDX41. TAILVAR has been used to

applied to all stop-loss substitution variants represented in
this table.

PM4 criteria for in-frame delins confined to the stop codon,
with no alteration of other C-terminal residues.
In-frame indel including stop codon (DDX41 PM4 decision
tree Figure, right): The MM-VCEP recommends applying PM4

criteria to in-frame indels involving the canonical stop codon,
with minimal alteration of adjacent C-terminal residues.

Specifications:
After analyzing the frequency of phenotypes amongst our DDX41 cohort,
we found that DDX41-related hematopoieticmalignancies more commonly
present as myeloid malignancies, causing MDS or AML, and much less
frequently lymphoid malignancies. For this reason, our phenotypic

definition of DDX41-related malignancies is based on the occurrence of
MDS/AML.
The MM-VCEP modified BA1 using extremely conservative values to
account for the unknown prevalence and disease attribution to DDX41. To
obtain a DDX41-specific population allele frequency for BA1, we utilized
the Whiffin/Ware calculator (http://cardiodb.org/allelefrequencyapp/) with
a prevalence of AML of 1 in 3650, as calculated from the SEER database
2023. We used conservative estimates for the following values:
penetrance estimate of 50% (PMID: 36322930), an allelic heterogeneity of
100% (conservative assumption that one pathogenic DDX41 variant would
account for 100% of cases), and a maximum genetic heterogeneity of
100% (conservative assumption that DDX41 – and no other gene –
accounts for 100% of cases). A 95% confidence interval was used to
develop the threshold.
Following the Whiffin/Ware calculation, the calculated threshold of
0.000274 was increased by two orders of magnitude, to provide
separation from the PM2_Supporting threshold. The threshold developed
for application of BA1 as a stand-alone criterion is a minor allele
frequency (MAF) of equal to or higher than ≥ 0.0274 (2.74%).
MM-VCEP Notes:
1. The MM-VCEP adopted the SVI recommendation that the variant be
present in any general continental population dataset with a
minimum number of 2,000 alleles and variant present in ≥ 5 alleles.
2. For founder variants, the MM-VCEP recommends ancestry matching
to the appropriate population group within gnomAD, or to use a
population database that is ancestry-matched, such as ToGoVar.

1. Comparison to a control is necessary;
2. Patient RNA data are better than minigene assays;

PAXgene tubes), particularly when the predicted effect is
nonsense-mediated decay, are used;

5. Quantification of the effect by SNP analysis is better than
percent splicing index (PSI) analysis is better than capillary
electrophoresis, which in turn, is better than estimation by gel
band density;
6. Multiple studies are better than a single study;

the splicing impact is incomplete (i.e., "leaky" splice site) or
unclear.
