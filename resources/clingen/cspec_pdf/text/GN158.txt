Criteria & Strength Specifications

PVS1
Original ACMG
Summary
Null variant (nonsense, frameshift, canonical +/−1 or 2 splice sites, initiation codon,
single or multi-exon deletion) in a gene where loss of function (LOF) is a known
mechanism of disease.

Caveats:
• Beware of genes where LOF is not a known disease mechanism (e.g. GFAP, MYH7).
• Use caution interpreting LOF variants at the extreme 3’ end of a gene.
• Use caution with splice variants that are predicted to lead to exon skipping but leave
the remainder of the protein intact.
• Use caution in the presence of multiple transcripts.

Very Strong
The Galactosemia VCEP will utilize SVI’s PVS1 recommendation for determining
applicable PVS1 strength level (Abou Tayoun et al 2018; PMID: 30192042). Refer to
the modified PVS1 decision tree attached.
Exons 6, 7, and 9 are in-frame. Exon skipping consequences for splice site variants
are listed in the attachment as well.

Original ACMG
Summary
Same amino acid change as a previously established pathogenic variant regardless of
nucleotide change.
Example: Val->Leu caused by either G>C or G>T in the same codon.
Caveat: Beware of changes that impact splicing rather than at the amino acid/protein
level.

PS2
Original ACMG
Summary

De novo (both maternity and paternity confirmed) in a patient with the disease and no
family history.
Note: Confirmation of paternity only is insufficient. Egg donation, surrogate motherhood,
errors in embryo transfer, etc. can contribute to non-maternity.

Not Applicable
Comments:
Because there has only ever been one reported de novo occurrence of a
GALT variant and de novo occurrence is not expected to significantly
contribute to disease, do novo data will be used in the PM6 code.

PS3
Original ACMG
Summary
Well-established in vitro or in vivo functional studies supportive of a damaging effect on
the gene or gene product.
Note: Functional studies that have been validated and shown to be reproducible and
robust in a clinical diagnostic laboratory setting are considered the most well-established.

Supporting
Applicable for up to 20% enzyme activity in a model system
Modification Disease-specific
Type:

PS4
Original ACMG
Summary
The prevalence of the variant in affected individuals is significantly increased compared to
the prevalence in controls.
Note 1: Relative risk (RR) or odds ratio (OR), as obtained from case-control studies, is
>5.0 and the confidence interval around the estimate of RR or OR does not include 1.0.
See manuscript for detailed guidance.
Note 2: In instances of very rare variants where case-control studies may not reach
statistical significance, the prior observation of the variant in multiple unrelated patients
with the same phenotype, and its absence in controls, may be used as moderate level of
evidence.

Not Applicable

PM2
Original ACMG
Summary
Absent from controls (or at extremely low frequency if recessive) in Exome Sequencing
Project, 1000 Genomes or Exome Aggregation Consortium.
Caveat: Population data for indels may be poorly called by next generation sequencing.
VCEP
PM2 threshold was calculated as one order of magnitude below BS1.
Specifications:

Supporting
Can only be used at the supporting level
Use the PopMax filtering allele frequency (FAF) from gnomAD.
Use if variant is present at ≤ 0.0005 or 0.05%
One order of magnitude below BS1
PM2 is not considered conflicting data with BP4 or BP7
A curated list of GALT variants known to be pathogenic despite not fulfilling the PM2
criterion has been developed (see PM2 exception list)

PM5
Original ACMG
Summary
Novel missense change at an amino acid residue where a different missense change
determined to be pathogenic has been seen before.
Example: Arg156His is pathogenic; now you observe Arg156Cys.
Caveat: Beware of changes that impact splicing rather than at the amino acid/protein
level.

Moderate

Splicing abnormalities (using VCEP-specified prediction algorithms or evidence from
literature) should be excluded for all missense variants
PM5 will be applied at moderate strength for 1 pathogenic variant with no benign
variation at the residue
The other variant(s) used for evidence should also have been curated using the GALT
VCEP rule specifications and must reach a pathogenic/likely pathogenic classification
without PM5.
Grantham should be used to compare the variants. The new variant must be equal or
worse than the known pathogenic variant.

PM6
Original ACMG
Summary
Assumed de novo, but without confirmation of paternity and maternity.

Moderate
Paternity and maternity must be confirmed. Patient must meet PP4.
Because there has only ever been one reported de novo occurrence of a GALT variant and
de novo occurrence is not expected to significantly contribute to disease, the VCEP will
use PM6 and not PS2. PM6 will not be upgraded with number of occurrences.

PP2
Original ACMG
Summary
Missense variant in a gene that has a low rate of benign missense variation and where
missense variants are a common mechanism of disease.

Not Applicable
Comments:
GALT is not constrained for missense (Z score: 0.91) variants.

PP3
Original ACMG
Summary
Multiple lines of computational evidence support a deleterious effect on the gene or gene
product (conservation, evolutionary, splicing impact, etc.).
Caveat: As many in silico algorithms use the same or very similar input for their
predictions, each algorithm should not be counted as an independent criterion. PP3 can
be used only once in any evaluation of a variant.

Supporting
For missense variants use REVEL with a score ≥0.7

In-frame in/dels, Provean (“deleterious”) and MutationTaster (“Disease-causing”)
must agree
For non-canonical splicing variants use SpliceAI with a score ≥0.2, look for cryptic
splice sites.

PP4
Original ACMG
Summary
Patient’s phenotype or family history is highly specific for a disease with a single genetic
etiology.

Moderate
RBC GALT enzyme activity <10%
In order to apply PP4 at the moderate level, newborn screen alone would not count,
must have confirmatory testing.
For PP4 to be applied at moderate level, full GALT gene sequencing (exons and

Variants must meet PM2_Supporting for PP4 to apply at any strength.

BS4
Original ACMG
Summary
Lack of segregation in affected members of a family.
Caveat: The presence of phenocopies for common phenotypes (i.e. cancer, epilepsy) can
mimic lack of segregation among affected individuals. Also, families may have more than
one pathogenic variant contributing to an autosomal dominant disorder, further
confounding an apparent lack of segregation.

Strong
Individual considered unaffected RBC GALT enzyme activity ≥25% with the caveat of no
bone marrow transfusions in individual.

BP4
Original ACMG
Summary
Multiple lines of computational evidence suggest no impact on gene or gene product
(conservation, evolutionary, splicing impact, etc)
Caveat: As many in silico algorithms use the same or very similar input for their
predictions, each algorithm cannot be counted as an independent criterion. BP4 can be
used only once in any evaluation of a variant.

Supporting
For missense variants use REVEL with a score of <0.15
In-frame in/dels, Provean (“neutral”) and MutationTaster (“polymorphism”) must
agree
For non-canonical splicing variants use SpliceAI with a score of <0.1, do not use if there is
evidence of a cryptic splice site

BP7
Original ACMG
Summary
A synonymous variant for which splicing prediction algorithms predict no impact to the
splice consensus sequence nor the creation of a new splice site AND the nucleotide is not
highly conserved.

Supporting
Use “as is”, but no conservation requirement
BP4 must be applied for BP7 to be used, the codes can be combined
Do not use for silent variants in the first nucleotide or last 3 nucleotides of the exon
Can also be used for intronic variants at or beyond positions +7/-21 with no
predicted effect on splicing.
